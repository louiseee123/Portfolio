01 — About me

An IT graduate who likes shipping things people actually use.

I care about the details most people never notice: clear hierarchy, fast load times, sensible defaults, and interfaces that don't make someone think twice.

The short version

I'm John Louise — an IT graduate from Consolatrix College of Toledo City. My path into development started with curiosity about how websites work and grew into a habit of building complete products: database, backend logic, interface, and deployment.

During my internship at the Toledo City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO), I moved from classroom projects to tools that staff depend on daily — a public resilience portal, a training-unit logbook, and a situational reporting system used during typhoon operations.

Fieldnote — Situational Report Maker

Toledo City CDRRMO · Operations & Warning

A secure reporting workspace for disaster-response teams. Offices publish situational reports in one place, attach photo evidence, and route updates through a clear review status — so decision-makers read the same picture during an emergency.

  • Dedicated channels per office — Ops & Warning, Admin & Training, Logistics
  • SITREP numbering with submitted / noted review states and urgency tags
  • Photo evidence attachments and structured incident metadata
  • Installable app shell, status filters, and approved-members-only access

CBTU — Capacity Building & Training Unit

Personnel & partner training logbook

A records system for the unit that trains the city's responders. It keeps a single, trustworthy log of who has been trained, which partners delivered the sessions, and what each person is certified to do — replacing scattered spreadsheets and paper forms.

  • Staff-only sign-in with administrator-created accounts
  • Central registry of trained individuals and partner organisations
  • Training history at a glance for audits and reporting
  • Clean, low-friction UI with light & dark themes

03 — Internship

Where the real work started.

On-the-Job Training at the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO), Toledo City — the place where my projects stopped being school exercises.

I joined CDRRMO as an IT intern and ended up building tools the office still uses: the public resilience portal, the CBTU training logbook, and the Fieldnote situational reporting system. Between deployments I handled video production for preparedness campaigns, mapped the city's risk areas, and kept office machines running.

The biggest lesson was learning to design with the people who use the software — watching a duty officer use a form is the fastest way to find out it's wrong.
